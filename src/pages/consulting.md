The starting point is not a product or a predetermined solution. It is the problem itself.

<ol class="flow" aria-label="Approach">
  <li>Investigate</li>
  <li>Diagnose</li>
  <li>Advise</li>
</ol>

We investigate how a system actually works, identify the underlying problem, and recommend the most appropriate course of action. That may involve new technology, existing technology, automation, a specialist contractor—or sometimes doing nothing.

We do not sell a particular technology or implementation service, so recommendations are independent of the products or contractors ultimately selected.

## Client-side technical advice

Organisations often have to rely on suppliers to tell them what technology they need. This creates an obvious problem when the supplier also profits from implementing the recommended solution.

Industrial Automata can act as an independent technical representative for the client:

<ol class="flow" aria-label="Client-side engagement">
  <li>Client</li>
  <li>Independent assessment</li>
  <li>Specification</li>
  <li>Contractor</li>
  <li>Implementation</li>
</ol>

The contractor remains responsible for doing the technical work. We ensure that the work being commissioned is actually appropriate to the client's requirements.

This can include helping organisations select contractors, assess proposals, define requirements and oversee implementation.

## Archaeology

The same investigative approach is applicable to archaeological projects.

Industrial Automata can provide independent archaeological and technical advice where archaeological knowledge, data, technology and project requirements intersect.

This includes archaeological systems, data, technology assessment, research methodology and translation between archaeological and technical disciplines.

## The underlying principle

Don't start by asking what technology to buy. Start by establishing what problem needs to be solved.

A good solution may be sophisticated.

It may also be a spreadsheet, a small Python program, an existing piece of software, a change to the workflow—or no new technology at all.
